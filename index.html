I implemented DeepMind’s Dreamer 4 recipe in PyTorch and tested whether training a policy inside a learned simulator beats behavior cloning when the simulator, its reward model, and the starting policy all come from a single fixed dataset. The answer depends on which finetune run you start from — that finding is the paper.

Paper

Does Imagination Help Offline? A Verified Empirical Study of Imagination-Based Policy Optimization versus Behavior Cloning

2026 · single author · manuscript

A world model is a learned simulator an agent can practice in instead of the real environment. Whether that practice helps when everything is learned from one fixed dataset is unsettled. I tested it on one simple control task — catching a ball in a cup — evaluating every policy on the same 500 real episodes. From one checkpoint, practicing in imagination helped modestly and consistently. But retraining that checkpoint with the same data and recipe swung the outcome from a large gain to a large loss, and none of the offline metrics I measured reliably predicted which you would get. Ten real episodes did.

  • +5.9 points of catch rate from imagination training over its behavior-cloning checkpoint, the weakest BC baseline in the study (95% CI [+1.5, +10.4]; 6/6 runs positive) — conditional on that one checkpoint.
  • −32.6 to +24.2 points across three retrains of the finetune with data and pretrained base held bitwise fixed (−32.6, −8.3, +24.2). Which retrain you start from explains ≈99% of the variance in deployed outcome (ICC 0.99, 95% CI [0.89, 1.00]).
  • 10 real episodes expose the collapsing run that no offline metric I measured reliably flagged in advance: its policy learns to occupy states its own frozen reward model misgrades, and the reward model’s calibration error on those trajectories separates it from every healthy policy with no overlap.

Offline, the world-model checkpoint is the dominant random factor. Imagination-trained policies should not be selected or deployed without a small real-environment audit.

How I work

  • verified Component-wise: λ-return and PMPO algebra unit-tested; a gradient-isolation test checks that the agent token cannot leak into world-model predictions; closed-loop evaluation is seed-deterministic on fixed hardware (90/90 replayed episodes identical).
  • regenerable Every closed-loop statistic in the paper regenerates from the released per-episode logs with one script. The figure above was rendered from episodes checked against those logs.
  • corrected in public A six-run study superseded my own single-run null, and the README keeps that history visible. Smaller errors are fixed in commits that say what was wrong — for example c116865, a ranking test rebuilt as an exact permutation test.
